title: Character design and casting for AI-rendered video

# Characters are specification, not vibes

An AI-rendered character exists only as its written specification: gender, age range,
ethnicity, build, hair, face details, wardrobe, body language, and one memorable detail.
Vague specs ("a friendly woman") produce a different person every clip; complete specs
rendered identically every time produce a cast. Write each character once, completely, and
reuse the exact wording everywhere — consistency is a copy-paste discipline.

# The memorable detail

Every principal character needs one specific, renderable identifier that survives every shot:
a red enamel ring, a faded denim jacket with a pin, reading glasses pushed into grey hair, a
tattoo peeking from a rolled sleeve. It anchors recognition across clips, gives close-ups a
subject, and is the single best defense against character drift between AI generations.

# Cast small

Short-form video supports 1-3 speaking characters; each additional face costs recognition
time the story can't spare. One protagonist carries most formats. Crowds and extras are set
dressing — keep them background, faceless, and out of focus. If two supporting characters
serve the same function, merge them.

# Casting for the audience

Characters are audience mirrors or audience aspirations. Match the protagonist's age, context
and texture to the target viewer ("a 30-something Colombo shop owner", not "a person") — or
deliberately cast the aspiration (who the viewer wants to be). Ethnicity, wardrobe and setting
should feel native to the market the video targets, not defaulted to a generic western stock
look.

# Body language is character

Specify the default physicality: how they stand, what their hands do, their resting
expression, their energy level. "Talks with her hands, leans in when excited" renders; "has a
warm personality" does not. Emotional beats become physical instructions: nervousness = a
fidgeting object, pride = squared shoulders and a slow exhale, defeat = a long look down before
speaking.

# Wardrobe is continuity currency

One outfit per character per continuous scene, described down to color and layer. A wardrobe
change is a scene change and must be story-motivated (time passed, occasion changed). Choose
wardrobe colors that serve the video's palette system and separate the character from the
background.
