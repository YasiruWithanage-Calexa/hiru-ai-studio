# What a Hiru shot is

Hiru AI Studio makes television: horizontal 16:9 pictures, organised as drama, episodes, scenes
and shots. Every shot is rendered by Veo 3.1, so a shot is 4, 6 or 8 seconds long and no other
length. A scene is planned as a set of such shots; the length of the scene comes from the script,
not from a template, and the same planning serves every Hiru format, not only dramas.

# Only the spoken line is Sinhala

Characters speak Sinhala. Everything around the speech (the shot description, the camera, the
sound notes, the planning) is written in English. The spoken line is kept exactly as written:
Sinhala conjuncts depend on the joiner characters inside a word, so a line is never retyped,
transliterated or "tidied". When a line is quoted back to a writer it is quoted whole.

# A line has to fit its shot

People speak at about 2.2 words a second, unhurried. A whispered, halting or trembling delivery
slows to about 1.8. That gives a rough ceiling per shot:

| Shot | Words it holds |
|---|---|
| 4 s | 8 |
| 6 s | 13 |
| 8 s | 17 |

Veo does not speed a line up to fit: it cuts the line off in the middle of a word. When a line is
too long for its shot there are only three honest fixes: give it a longer shot, split it across two
shots at a natural pause, or let a writer shorten it. Software never trims a line itself, because
which half of a sentence a scene needs is a writing decision. It reports the overrun and the
person decides.

# Sound is one decision per shot

Each shot carries its meaning through exactly one of: dialogue, voice-over, song, ambient sound
only, music only, or deliberate silence. A silent shot has to be stated as silent in the prompt, or
Veo will invent speech for it. A shot that is not spoken is never padded with words to look fuller.

# Keeping a drama consistent

A character, a place or a prop keeps its look across shots and episodes, so the studio writes it
down once as canon (for a character, their look and wardrobe; for a place, how it looks) and every shot refers back to
that card instead of describing the person again in new words. When the canon changes, shots that
were made against the old version are reported as out of date; they are not silently redone.
