# What Veo makes four, six or eight seconds of

Veo renders clips of 4, 6 or 8 seconds. A line is spoken at about 2.2 words a second and is never trimmed: a line that cannot be said inside the take is reported to a person. A card therefore holds its pictures and speech on one clock, with half a second before the first word and half a second after the last. A line never spans two takes.

# What renders well

Faces and slow expressions. Slow push-ins and slow pull-outs. Hands on simple props (one hand, one object, a slow action). Shallow depth of field. Golden-hour and lamp light. A locked-off wide of a place. A single person in a clear light. Standing up, sitting down, walking away on a plain path.

# What is risky

Readable text on a screen, a sign, a letter or a phone (it renders as gibberish). Mirrors and reflections. Fast or complicated hand work (typing, counting money, sewing, playing an instrument). Crowds and more than three sharp faces in one frame. Two people touching (limbs merge). Rack focus (say the order plainly and keep to one pull). A reveal by camera move (one direction, name where it ends). Matching two shots frame for frame.

# Workarounds

Text on a screen: show the person's reaction instead, or add the text in post. Mirrors: shoot the person, not the reflection. Fast hands: cut to the face or keep the hands slow. Touching: frame the contact from behind or in a close-up of one hand. Crowds: keep two or three faces sharp and the rest soft or silhouetted. Off-speaker lines: use the same voice description for the person in every shot, so the voice matches their on-screen shots. Names on phones and signs: add them in post.

# What a card writes for Veo

Say what the camera sees and what moves, in the order it happens. Performance is visible behaviour (the smile stays a beat after her eyes change), never a feeling word. One camera move per card. One person's change per card where possible. State the sound plainly (ambience drains to her breath; silence).
