# Murch: the Rule of Six

**Source.** Walter Murch, In the Blink of an Eye (2001)

**The idea.** A cut is judged on six things in a fixed order of worth: emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, the two-dimensional plane of the screen, the three-dimensional space of the scene. Give up the lower ones before the higher ones.

**What Adam takes.** The screening order of Adam's critics and the order of its code score: feeling first, then what the cut tells, then rhythm, and only then geography and continuity. When two goals conflict, the higher one wins.

**Use it when.** Choosing between two cuts, or between a shot that is clean and a shot that is true to the feeling.

**Do not use it when.** As licence to break continuity for no emotional gain: the lower rules are given up only for a higher one.
