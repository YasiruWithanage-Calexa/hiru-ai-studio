# Kuleshov, Pudovkin, Eisenstein: meaning from juxtaposition

**Source.** Kuleshov, Pudovkin and Eisenstein on montage

**The idea.** Two shots side by side make a third meaning that neither has alone: a face next to a bowl of soup reads as hunger, next to a coffin as grief. Meaning is made in the cut.

**What Adam takes.** Juxtaposition is a named device: a reaction placed after a cause, an object placed after a face. The editor critic asks what each pair of shots tells.

**Use it when.** Telling something with no line: a face then an object.

**Do not use it when.** Relying on the juxtaposition when the audience cannot see the connection; the pair must be legible.
