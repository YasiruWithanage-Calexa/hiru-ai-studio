# Hitchcock and Truffaut: size is importance, and suspense

**Source.** Hitchcock/Truffaut (1967)

**The idea.** The size of the subject in the frame should match its importance to the story at that moment. Suspense comes from the audience knowing something a character does not (the bomb under the table).

**What Adam takes.** Size follows weight: the closer the shot, the more the moment matters. The dramaturg lists what the audience knows that a character does not, and the treatment makes use of it.

**Use it when.** Choosing a shot size, or deciding what to show a viewer before a character learns it.

**Do not use it when.** Close-ups on every line: when everything is large, nothing is important.
