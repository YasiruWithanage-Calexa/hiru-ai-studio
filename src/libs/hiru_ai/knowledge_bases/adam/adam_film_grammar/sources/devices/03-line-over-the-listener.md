# The line over the listener

**Ask it as.** Whose face should the camera be on while someone speaks? How do I show how a line lands? Should every line be on the speaker?

**What it does to the viewer.** Plays a line over the face that hears it, so the drama is in the reception, not the delivery (the cut is on the reaction, not the sentence).

**Use it when.** A line that hurts, flatters, accuses or confesses; a long speech where the listener's face is the story. The speaker must be in the scene.

**Do not use it when.** When lip sync matters (a clear mouth in the shot), when the listener has no reaction to show, or for a line whose speaker is the subject of the frame. Never use it for every line: it is a choice.

**Allowed kind.** listener (elaboration, on the card as a line off its speaker)

**Veo feasibility.** good, if the speaker is not visible; the voice is placed off screen. Use the same voice description for that person in their on-screen shots.

**On a card.** On the card, lines: [{line_id, on_speaker: false, over_whom: the listener}]. The script moment that carries the line stays script; the picture is the listener's face (CU or MCU, shallow focus).

**Example (a Hiru-style scene).** The father's offer of money plays over Kasun's still face, because his resisting it is what we have come to watch.
