# The smile that outlives the eyes

**Ask it as.** How do I show someone hiding pain? How do I show a character pretending to be fine? What does a restrained reaction look like?

**What it does to the viewer.** Splits the face: the mouth keeps the social smile for a beat after the eyes have taken the news. The viewer sees both the mask and what it covers.

**Use it when.** Restraint scenes: a daughter told to obey, a man receiving a verdict in public, anyone who must not show it. Works best in a close-up with a push-in.

**Do not use it when.** When the character has no reason to hide, or when the scene needs the feeling out loud. Do not stack it with a second reaction device in the same shot.

**Allowed kind.** performance inside a script moment (no added moment)

**Veo feasibility.** risky. Veo tends to move the whole face at once. Write the order of the change in the beat (eyes first, mouth a beat later) and keep the shot to one person and one change.

**On a card.** Inside the turn's script moment, a card beat: window [4, 8], action: the word lands, performance: the smile stays a beat after her eyes change. CU, long lens, slow push_in.

**Example (a Hiru-style scene).** Anudi hears the word marriage on the phone: the smile stays on her lips while her eyes drop.
