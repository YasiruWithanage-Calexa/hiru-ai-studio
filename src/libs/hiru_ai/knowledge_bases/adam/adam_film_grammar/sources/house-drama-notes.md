# Hiru house drama notes

**Status: a first draft by Claude from the plan and the 3-episode drama bible. These are working assumptions for the director agents until the person (and a Hiru director) corrects them. Do not treat any line below as settled house policy.**

# Family authority and respect

In the family dramas the elder's word carries weight: a father's decision is rarely argued in the open. Respect shows in address (Thaththe, sir) and in what a young person does not say. Play refusal and resentment as restraint: a lowered gaze, a held breath, an obedient line said quietly, not a raised voice. Frame the elder with authority (a steady eye-level or slightly low angle, a still camera) and the young person with the cost of obeying (a close face, a small gesture of the hands).

# Marriage pressure

When a marriage is arranged or decided for a young woman, the drama is in what she hides while she agrees. The audience should see the mask and what is under it. Keep the news itself plain and let the reaction carry it. A cheerful companion who does not yet know is a strong source of irony: show the audience the truth before him.

# Restraint over melodrama

The audience of a teledrama expects the emotional beat to land on a face, held, often with a music sting. Adam may withhold the sting: a drained room and one breath can hit harder than a swell. Use the expected shape (a close-up on the reaction after a revelation) and surprise within it (a silence instead of a sting, a smile that outlives the eyes). Surprise only one thing per scene.

# The language of the lines

Spoken lines stay in the language the script was written in (Sinhala unless a person asked for a translation). A plan names a line by its id and never rewrites it. A card that puts a line over a listener must say whose face it plays over.

# Places and objects

The recurring places of the current drama are the roadside tea stall (a three-wheeler, a kettle, cups), the lake bench, an office, a family home and a new restaurant. Use what the place card gives; do not add a prop the place does not have. Tea, a phone, an envelope, a bag, a sign: ordinary objects do the work.

# Length

The current episodes are about a minute each. A scene that needs more time than the episode gives is reported, never squeezed. Prefer one clear idea per scene and let the treatment say how many seconds it needs.
