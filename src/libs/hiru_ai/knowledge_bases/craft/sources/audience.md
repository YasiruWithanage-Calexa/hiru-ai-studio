# The scroll-stop decision happens in 1.5 seconds

A viewer decides to stay or scroll before the second second ends. The opening frame + first
words must contain one of: motion already in progress, a face with a legible strong emotion, a
visual anomaly, a direct question, a bold claim, or a number. "Establishing" openings (logo,
slow fade, scenery, greeting) are scroll triggers. Design the hook first; the video exists to
pay off its hook.

# Hook patterns that work

Curiosity gap ("Nobody tells you what happens after you quit"), stakes-first ("I spent my last
20,000 rupees on this"), mid-action ("—so I said no, and her face just dropped"),
contradiction ("The worst review we ever got saved this shop"), enemy naming ("Your landlord
loves that you don't know this"), and pattern interrupt (visually wrong thing in a normal
scene). Compete hooks: draft three different patterns for the same video and pick the one with
the sharpest specific detail, not the cleverest wording.

# Retention is engineered, not hoped for

Map the retention curve: a promise at 0-2s, first payoff by 25% of runtime, a new open loop
before each payoff closes, an energy or visual pattern change every ~8-10 seconds, and the
biggest payoff last. Never resolve everything mid-video — a fully answered question is an exit
ramp. The middle third is where videos die: put the second-best moment there, not exposition.

# Platform native beats produced

Content that looks like an ad performs like an ad (skipped). Short-form thrives on perceived
authenticity: direct address, imperfect but intentional framing, real locations, specific
numbers and names. Polish belongs in the craft (lighting, story, sound), not in the surface
gloss. Vertical 9:16 always; captions/text assume sound-off first viewing; the first frame
must be legible as a thumbnail.

# Shareability triggers

People share what expresses their identity ("this is so us"), what gives them social currency
(surprising fact, insider knowledge), what they want a specific person to see (tag-a-friend
emotional payload), and what makes them feel something strong enough to need company. Design
the share moment deliberately — usually the ending line — and ask: who exactly forwards this,
and to whom?

# Goal → format mapping

Awareness goals want emotion-first storytelling with the brand as setting, not subject.
Trust/authority goals want expert breakdowns, behind-the-scenes, or origin stories.
Conversion goals want transformation proof (before/after, testimonial-shaped narrative) with
one concrete next step. Mismatching goal and format is the most common noob strategy error —
fix the format before polishing the content.
