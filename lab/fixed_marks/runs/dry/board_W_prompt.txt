A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 6 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 2 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same scene's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel. The weather and the ground (dry or wet, rain falling or not) are as each panel says: they can change during the scene, and a panel that says it is dry has no rain and no wet ground.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location sheet for Colombo roadside: six named views of the one place, numbered left to right, then top to bottom: view 1 Wide; view 2 Looking left; view 3 Looking right; view 4 Reverse; view 5 Detail 1; view 6 Detail 2; image 2 is the vehicle sheet for Food three-wheeler: six named views of the one vehicle; image 3 is the approved last panel of the previous part of this scene's board. Take from image 1 the place itself: its buildings, its fixed things and every place that stands in it, their materials and where each one stands. Every panel below names the view its background comes from: paint that view's background, the same things on the same sides, seen from the panel's own camera (closer or tighter as the panel says). Never copy the sheet's gutters or layout. Take from image 2: ONLY the vehicle itself, exactly as shown: its make and model, body shape and colour, wheels, lights and windows, and its inside: its dashboard, steering and seats. Ignore everything around the vehicle in that picture (its street, ground and light); image 3: panel 1 of this sheet, repeated exactly: the same camera, place, light and mannequins. Image 4 is the blocking plate for shots 1 to 15: one still of this place from view 1's anchor camera, the people standing on their ground marks. Take from image 4 only where each person stands on the ground, what stands behind each of them and the gap between them; every panel of shots 1 to 15 keeps them on exactly these spots, seen from its own camera. Not its framing, its light or its poses.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Kasun is the red mannequin; Anudi is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. The place, the light and the weather are a real, photorealistic film still.

Props (every panel): every prop (phone) and every small thing a person holds or carries is a plain, smooth, matte white clay stand-in of the real object: its real size and outline, and nothing else: no screen, buttons, logo, lettering, pattern or colour. A vehicle is never a clay stand-in: every vehicle is painted real, exactly as its vehicle plate shows it, with its real colours. Each stand-in is held, carried or set down exactly as the real thing would be. The place itself is real: its ground, walls, water, plants, what is built into it, and Colombo roadside, Food three-wheeler, painted as a real photograph, never as clay.

World (every panel): Early-morning Colombo in soft overcast daylight, with fine rain falling and wet asphalt and concrete. Road light comes from camera right, with gentle shelter shade and a slightly darker vending bay; use restrained roadside greens, grey concrete, earth colours and soft highlights without a grade change. All movable props are real-size, plain matte white clay stand-ins without markings. The older compact maroon food three-wheeler remains real, with its black vinyl roof, faded panels, dark-brown trim, worn silver fittings, hinged service hatch and scratched stainless-steel counter.

At the left end of this stretch, the blue-awning vending bay holds the food three-wheeler, with its front cab pointing left and its open service compartment behind Kasun. Kasun's conversational mark is left of Anudi's; the pavement continues from her mark toward the right alongside the pale weathered boundary wall and recessed shutters. The square road-facing setups keep the vending bay left and the boundary wall right. The angled singles from Kasun's end look right along the pavement, placing the receding boundary wall on the left of those compositions. Every camera stays on the road-facing side of the conversational axis.

Ground marks (shots 1 to 15): nobody walks in these shots, so each person stands on ONE spot on the ground for all of them, and every panel of them shows them on that same spot, seen from its own camera. Small movements (a turn of the head or body, a lean, a shift of weight, half a step) stay within half a metre of the mark; the feet never travel further. Kasun (the red mannequin) stands on the concrete pavement half a metre in from the kerb drain, one step to the right of the rear of the food three-wheeler, whose open service hatch and the blue awning above it are directly behind him and to his left. Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Bodies and things (every panel): every mannequin has exactly two arms and two hands, each hand where the panel says. A held stand-in is gripped by the hand the panel names, its handle or edge inside the closed fingers; nothing hangs or floats in the air. Feet stand flat on the ground the panel names. A bag hangs from the shoulder or forearm the panel names.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
