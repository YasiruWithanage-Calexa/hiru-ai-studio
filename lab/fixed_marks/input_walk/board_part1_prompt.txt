A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 5 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 2 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same moment's world, painted together: the same people, props, weather, light and colour grade in every panel.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location plate for Colombo roadside; image 2 is the reference sheet for phone. Take from image 1: the kind of place: its ground, water, plants, structures and materials. It is ONE view of the place; each panel has its own camera position, so do not repeat this view in every panel; image 2: phone exactly as shown.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Kasun is the red mannequin; Anudi is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the props, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

World (every panel): One image contains five equal portrait 9:16 panels, ordered left to right across the top row, then left to right across the bottom row; the bottom-right grid space stays blank. Use narrow plain gutters without text or symbols. Kasun is a smooth, featureless red artist's mannequin of medium height and lean build; Anudi is a smooth, featureless blue mannequin of average height and slender build. Props and surroundings are painted realistically. Fine drizzle, rain-wet asphalt and concrete, soft overcast morning light and faint amber shop illumination share one naturalistic, slightly desaturated grade. Anudi holds an open navy umbrella in her left hand and carries a structured tan handbag on her right shoulder, with brass-coloured fittings and an open exterior pocket. The orange smartphone has a transparent case and three prominent rear camera circles. The parked three-wheeler has muted dark-green bodywork, a black canopy and a dull metal rear wheel. Nothing bears readable lettering.

Geography: The walking route runs across the image from left to right, with every camera remaining on the road side of that axis and looking toward the pavement and buildings. The three-wheeler occupies the left end of a compact vending bay beneath a blue awning; its rear wheel stands beside the open kerb drain and the phone's landing patch. Beyond the vehicle, the narrow concrete pavement continues right alongside a weathered pale boundary wall toward recessed shop shutters. Anudi passes the vehicle and continues right; Kasun starts beside it and follows her rightward. The blue awning remains on frame left as it recedes, while the same boundary wall becomes more prominent toward frame right. The drain follows the lower edge of this connected route.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
