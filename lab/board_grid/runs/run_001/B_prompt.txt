A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 6 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 2 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same scene's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel. The weather and the ground (dry or wet, rain falling or not) are as each panel says: they can change during the scene, and a panel that says it is dry has no rain and no wet ground.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location plate for Colombo roadside; image 2 is the approved last panel of the previous part of this scene's board. Take from image 1: the kind of place: its ground, water, plants, structures and materials. It is ONE view of the place; each panel has its own camera position, so do not repeat this view in every panel; image 2: panel 1 of this sheet, repeated exactly: the same camera, place, light and mannequins.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Kasun is the red mannequin; Anudi is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the props, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

World (every panel): Use only their poses to convey attention. Render the setting and props realistically: Anudi's orange smartphone has a transparent case matching the reference, her navy folding umbrella is open, and her tan structured handbag has brass-coloured fittings. Throughout the conversation her left hand holds the umbrella shaft at waist height, with the handbag suspended from her left shoulder; her right hand receives and retains the phone. Soft early-morning overcast daylight comes from the road at camera right, balanced around 5600K, with warm naturalistic colour, muted greens and earth tones, and subdued amber shop practicals. The pavement and patched asphalt are wet, with shallow reflections and no falling rain throughout. Keep the business slightly darker than the foreground.

Geography: The narrow concrete pavement forms a continuous left-to-right path between the road in the foreground and weathered commercial frontages behind it. A small, muted leaf-green food three-wheeler occupies the vending bay at the left end, with an open serving hatch, metal counter and shelter canopy. Immediately right of the bay is the conversation position: Kasun stands on its left, Anudi on its right. A pale-green building pier and weathered concrete boundary wall continue behind Anudi toward the open pavement at screen right, her departure direction. The kerb drain follows the pavement across the foreground; utility poles and cables belong to the same connected frontage. All cameras stay on the road-facing side of the conversational axis. Wider views show the business at frame left and the pale-green pier at frame right; tighter views preserve those sides without forcing distant landmarks into their crops.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
