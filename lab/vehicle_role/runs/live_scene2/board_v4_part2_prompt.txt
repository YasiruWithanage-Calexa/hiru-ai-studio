A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 2 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 2 panels across and 1 down, read left to right. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same scene's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel. The weather and the ground (dry or wet, rain falling or not) are as each panel says: they can change during the scene, and a panel that says it is dry has no rain and no wet ground.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location plate for Colombo roadside; image 2 is the approved last panel of the previous part of this scene's board. Take from image 1: the kind of place: its ground, water, plants, structures and materials. It is ONE view of the place; each panel has its own camera position, so do not repeat this view in every panel; image 2: panel 1 of this sheet, repeated exactly: the same camera, place, light and mannequins.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Anudi is the red mannequin; driver is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. The place, the light and the weather are a real, photorealistic film still.

Props (every panel): every prop (handbag, phone, envelope, bag, umbrella) and every small thing a person holds or carries is a plain, smooth, matte white clay stand-in of the real object: its real size and outline, and nothing else: no screen, buttons, logo, lettering, pattern or colour. A vehicle is never a clay stand-in: every vehicle is painted real, exactly as its vehicle plate shows it, with its real colours. Each stand-in is held, carried or set down exactly as the real thing would be. The place itself is real: its ground, walls, water, plants, what is built into it, and Colombo roadside, Food three-wheeler, painted as a real photograph, never as clay.

World (every panel): Soft, diffuse early-morning daylight, approximately 5600K, with neutral contrast, restrained Colombo street colours and gentle highlights. Their poses alone convey their attention. The setting has patched asphalt, narrow concrete pavement, open kerb drainage, weathered commercial buildings, concrete boundary walls, utility poles and overhead cables. Vehicle bodies, upholstery, doors and steering wheel remain real materials. Every carried object is a plain matte white clay stand-in matching the reference object's size and outline. Draw no lettering or other text.

Geography: The family car approaches and stops facing screen right, its pavement-side rear door nearest the roadside cameras. Anudi's route runs from the rear seat through that doorway, then screen right along the narrow pavement toward the office approach and the compact food-three-wheeler bay. The patched road and open kerb drain accompany the route on the street side; a stained concrete boundary wall with a green end pier provides a recognisable landmark at frame right across the successive exterior positions. The vending bay is farther along this same pavement and first enters the picture in shot 6. Keep the roadside cameras on Side A and all lateral travel screen right; show no person at the food three-wheeler.

PANEL 1 (row 1, column 1) — At the streetward edge of the pavement, retaining Side A and the screen-right route; standing head height, medium-wide three-quarter view, 40mm feel. Centre Anudi and reserve ample empty space above the folded umbrella. Background: The same stained concrete boundary wall and green pier continue at frame right, now seen from farther along the pavement; the kerb drain remains toward the lower-left edge. The real small food three-wheeler first appears softly at frame right, farther along the route, its wheels supported on the asphalt vending bay. Dry pavement and dry asphalt, no rain in this opening frame; no person is visible at the business. The red mannequin stands stationary near centre with both feet planted on the concrete, chest facing screen right in three-quarter profile and head tipped slightly upward in that direction. Its left hand grips the folded clay umbrella's curved handle, holding the closed shaft upright on the near side at centre-left, with clear space above it. Its right hand retains the clay phone on the far side at centre-right, and the clay handbag rests against the near hip from its left-shoulder strap. Hands: the red mannequin: left hand Grips the folded clay umbrella's curved handle, shaft upright on the near side, centre-left; the clay handbag strap rests on the left shoulder; right hand Grips the clay phone at waist level on the far side, centre-right.

Include the entire umbrella, Anudi at frame left and the three-wheeler centre-right. Background: The real food three-wheeler occupies centre-right, all its wheels on the asphalt vending bay beside the pavement; show its exterior without a view into the business. Its left hand grips the clay umbrella's curved handle on the near side, supporting the fully open canopy above its body; the clay handbag hangs from its left shoulder against the near hip. Its right hand grips the clay phone on the far side just ahead of the torso, while the real food three-wheeler stands centre-right with clear pavement between it and the mannequin. Hands: the red mannequin: left hand Grips the open clay umbrella's curved handle on the near side, frame left; the clay handbag strap rests on the left shoulder; right hand Grips the clay phone on the far side just ahead of the torso, toward the right edge of the figure.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Bodies and things (every panel): every mannequin has exactly two arms and two hands, each hand where the panel says. A held stand-in is gripped by the hand the panel names, its handle or edge inside the closed fingers; nothing hangs or floats in the air. Feet stand flat on the ground the panel names. A bag hangs from the shoulder or forearm the panel names.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
