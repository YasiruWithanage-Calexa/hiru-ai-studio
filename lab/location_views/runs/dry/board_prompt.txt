A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 6 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 2 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same scene's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel. The weather and the ground (dry or wet, rain falling or not) are as each panel says: they can change during the scene, and a panel that says it is dry has no rain and no wet ground.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location sheet for Colombo roadside: six named views of the one place, numbered left to right, then top to bottom: view 1 Front wide; view 2 Looking left; view 3 Looking right; view 4 Reverse; view 5 Hatch detail; view 6 Ground detail. Take from image 1 the place itself: its buildings, the food three-wheeler, the wall, the pavement and the drain, their materials and where each one stands. Every panel below names the view its background comes from: paint that view's background, the same things on the same sides, seen from the panel's own camera (closer or tighter as the panel says). Never copy the sheet's gutters or layout.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Kasun is the red mannequin; Anudi is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. The place, the light and the weather are a real, photorealistic film still.

Props (every panel): every prop (phone) and every small thing a person holds or carries is a plain, smooth, matte white clay stand-in of the real object: its real size and outline, and nothing else: no screen, buttons, logo, lettering, pattern or colour. A vehicle that is a prop is a full-size white clay vehicle with its doors, windows and seats as shapes. Each stand-in is held, carried or set down exactly as the real thing would be. The place itself is real: its ground, walls, water, plants, what is built into it, and Colombo roadside, Food three-wheeler, painted as a real photograph, never as clay.

World (every panel): Soft early-morning overcast daylight on a Colombo roadside, with steady fine rain, wet concrete pavement and patched dark asphalt. Road light enters from camera right; the food-business shelter gives gentle shade, with restrained amber shop light deeper behind it. Use muted roadside greens, grey concrete and earth colours, controlled saturation and soft highlights without changing the grade between panels. Render them without human surface details or clothing. Every portable object is a plain matte white clay stand-in of its actual size and outline, without markings. The food three-wheeler, its serving hatch and shelter remain real parts of the location. Draw no text or graphic annotations.

Geography: The narrow pavement runs horizontally between the road in the foreground and weathered commercial frontages behind. Kasun's food three-wheeler occupies the vending bay at frame left, its open serving hatch beneath a compact shelter; a pale weathered boundary wall with a leaf-green building surface above continues along the pavement to frame right. An open kerb drain separates pavement from road. The conversation takes place immediately right of the vending bay, with Kasun left facing right and Anudi right facing left. The onward pavement leads screen right, while the short return to the business leads screen left. Every camera remains on the road-facing side of their conversational axis. The serving hatch and shelter remain the left-hand landmark, and the boundary wall remains the right-hand landmark through wider views, closer views and returns.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Bodies and things (every panel): every mannequin has exactly two arms and two hands, each hand where the panel says. A held stand-in is gripped by the hand the panel names, its handle or edge inside the closed fingers; nothing hangs or floats in the air. Feet stand flat on the ground the panel names. A bag hangs from the shoulder or forearm the panel names.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
