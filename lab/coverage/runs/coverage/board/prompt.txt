A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 8 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 3 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a landscape 16:9 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same moment's world, painted together: the same people, props, weather, light and colour grade in every panel.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Anudi is the red mannequin; Kasun is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the props, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

World (every panel): One painted storyboard image arranged three panels across and three down, read left to right, with eight occupied panels and the bottom-right cell left blank. Use plain panel borders without any lettering, numbers, symbols or captions. Daytime on a Colombo roadside in fine rain, with damp asphalt, shallow reflective patches and restrained rain-grey colour. Soft overcast light comes from the open road on the right, with deeper shade beneath the vehicle canopy on the left. Anudi is always the same smooth red artist's mannequin; Kasun is always the same smooth blue artist's mannequin, each with consistent adult proportions. Render the setting and props realistically: a small teal food three-wheeler with a charcoal canopy and metal service counter, an open black umbrella, a brown shoulder handbag with an outer pocket, and one black smartphone with an unreadable screen. No visible branding or lettering.

Geography: The roadside route runs across the image from left to right. The food three-wheeler occupies the starting zone on the left; roadside block 1 is immediately beyond it, block 2 is farther right along the pursuit route, and block 4 is the meeting point farther right again. All cameras remain on the same road-facing side of this travel line, looking across or obliquely along it, so both mannequins travel screen right. A pale concrete kerb and sparse tropical planting continue along the far side of the route. The three-wheeler is the main connecting landmark: it begins close on frame left and becomes smaller and farther left as the cameras advance toward block 4. Close views retain it on the left as a soft fragment rather than reversing the geography.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
