Accidents are coverage. When a block has a small thing dropped, slipped, passed, spilled or knocked over, or a person who must NOT notice or react, never ask one clip for all of it: plan it the way television dramas do, and let the cut join the clips. (1) An INSERT of the accident alone, 2 to 4 s: only the hand, the bag and the thing, the thing large in the frame, with a physical cause the camera sees (half into the pocket, it tips out as the bag swings), never just "it slips". (2) A separate wide or medium of the person moving on: its first frame ALREADY has the thing where it fell (the state ledger places it), so the video model never has to make it appear. In it the person always has something to do and somewhere specific to look (up the road for a three-wheeler, at the traffic), and leaves the frame at a stated second; never "without looking down", "does not notice" or an empty stretch, which the model plays as a pause that reads as noticing. (3) Where the story waits on the thing, a still clip of 2 s of the thing alone where it lies. Each clip has ONE action and ONE camera setup. In every clip's beats the action is what the people and things do and nothing else: never a camera word ("unchanged", "hold", "tableau", "the frame holds", "locked"), which belongs to cinematography only. Never write a first frame or a state the clip's action must then undo (an umbrella open in the first frame and closed in the text): the opening picture and the text agree.
