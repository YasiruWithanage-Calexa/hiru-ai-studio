A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 16 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 4 panels across and 4 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a landscape 16:9 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same moment's world, painted together: the same people, clothes, props, weather, light and colour grade in every panel.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location plate for Cash Van; image 2 is the reference sheet for phone. Take from image 1: the kind of place: its ground, water, plants, structures and materials. It is ONE view of the place; each panel has its own camera position, so do not repeat this view in every panel; image 2: phone exactly as shown.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Dinu is the red mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the props, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

World (every panel): Paint one unified storyboard image, four panels across and four down, in shot order, with plain gutters and no written marks anywhere. Dinu is a smooth, solid-red artist's mannequin of medium-tall, lean build; all human figures, including those inside the photograph, use the same smooth mannequin treatment. The compact right-hand-drive cash van has a dark grey moulded dashboard, worn black vinyl seats, rubber mats and a solid steel partition behind the cab. A thin, knotted red cotton thread remains around Dinu's left wrist. One slightly scuffed black smartphone stays secured upright on the central dashboard within left-hand reach, immediately left of the fixed, faded photograph. Rain beads cover the windows; cool blue-green instrument light and narrow sodium-orange light from the driver's side shape the cabin, with deep blacks and a restrained, uneasy grade. Props and surroundings are painted realistically; phone controls use simple symbols without lettering.

Geography: The road lies beyond the front windscreen, with the central dashboard beneath it, the steering wheel directly ahead of the right-hand driver's seat, and the steel partition behind both front seats. The empty passenger seat is on the left. Dinu remains in this single cabin zone throughout: the broad and oblique views look across from the passenger side, keeping him frame right and his forward direction toward screen left; the tight near-frontal views stand farther forward beside the windscreen and look back toward him, with the road just beyond the lens. Dashboard inserts look forward and right from the passenger side without crossing the driver's forward axis. The phone and photograph never exchange positions. Outside, wet Colombo streets continue along the original course through panel 14; panel 15 still opens before the steering change, while panel 16 belongs to the new course toward the industrial area.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
