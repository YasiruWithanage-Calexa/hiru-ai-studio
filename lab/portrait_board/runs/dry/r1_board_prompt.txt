A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 6 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 2 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same moment's world, painted together: the same people, props, weather, light and colour grade in every panel.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location plate for Food three-wheeler. Take from image 1: the kind of place: its ground, water, plants, structures and materials. It is ONE view of the place; each panel has its own camera position, so do not repeat this view in every panel.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Kasun is the red mannequin; office worker is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the props, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

World (every panel): One painted storyboard image contains six equal portrait panels, arranged three across and two down in shot order, with narrow plain gutters. All people are smooth artist’s mannequins: Kasun is red, medium-height and lean; the office worker is blue, shorter and compact; distant pedestrians are neutral grey. Early-morning Colombo has dry, coarse asphalt, soft daylight entering from frame right, gentle shadows and a slightly desaturated, warm naturalistic grade. The older maroon three-wheeler has a black vinyl roof, small dents, worn silver fittings, an open metal serving hatch and a narrow scratched stainless-steel ledge. Keep the same scuffed two-litre silver flask with its dark-brown handle, compact trays of golden triangular pastries, oval rolls and round cutlets, plain metal kettle and pale wiping cloth throughout. The customer uses one plain cream cup; filled tea is caramel-brown with a faint steam wisp. Vehicle windows obscure occupants. All signboards and vehicle plates are blank; draw no lettering, labels, numbers, captions or arrows.

Geography: The establishing position looks along two receding traffic lanes, with the near pavement and parked tuk-tuks on frame left and low shops and a bus stop on frame right. A broad dark-green tree beside the bus-stop pole is the connecting landmark, remaining on frame right when the view moves to the nearby food counter. The three-wheeler stands along the near curb, its cab toward the establishing position and its open serving hatch farther along the pavement. Counter cameras stand on the public side near the cab end, looking obliquely along the ledge toward the rear opening; maroon vehicle surfaces occupy the left while the road, bus-stop pole and tree appear through the right-hand opening. Kasun stays behind the ledge at frame left. The customer approaches from frame right facing left and later leaves across the frame toward screen right. Every counter view remains on this public side of their axis.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
