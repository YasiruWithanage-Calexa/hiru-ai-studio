A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 4 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 2 panels across and 2 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a tall portrait 9:16 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same moment's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location sheet for Colombo roadside: six named views of the one place, numbered left to right, then top to bottom: view 1 Wide; view 2 Looking left; view 3 Looking right; view 4 Reverse; view 5 Detail 1; view 6 Detail 2. Take from image 1 the place itself: its buildings, its fixed things, the pavement and the drain, their materials and where each one stands. Every panel below names the view its background comes from: paint that view's background, the same things on the same sides, seen from the panel's own camera (closer or tighter as the panel says). Never copy the sheet's gutters or layout. Image 2 is the vehicle sheet for Family car (a pearl-silver 2019 BMW 520d four-door sedan): six named views of the one car, numbered the same way: view 1 Front three-quarter; view 2 Side; view 3 Rear three-quarter; view 4 Inside from the rear; view 5 Inside from the front; view 6 Detail. Take from image 2: ONLY the vehicle itself, exactly as shown: its make and model, body shape and colour, wheels, lights and windows, and its inside: the charcoal dashboard, the right-hand steering wheel, the beige seats. Ignore everything around the vehicle in that picture (its floor, walls and light). Image 3 is the vehicle plate for Food three-wheeler (an older maroon three-wheeler with a black vinyl roof and a food counter hatch). Take from image 3: ONLY the vehicle itself, exactly as shown: its make and model, body shape and colour, wheels, lights and windows, its side hatch and serving ledge. Ignore everything around the vehicle in that picture (its floor, walls and light).

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Anudi is the red mannequin; driver is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the vehicles, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

Props (every panel): every prop (phone, handbag, umbrella) and every small thing a person holds or carries is a plain, smooth, matte white clay stand-in of the real object: its real size and outline, and nothing else. A vehicle is never a clay stand-in: every vehicle is painted real, exactly as its vehicle sheet or plate shows it, with its real colours. The place itself is real, painted as a real photograph, never as clay.

World (every panel): soft overcast early-morning daylight with a neutral, restrained grade, grey asphalt, dusty concrete and muted green surfaces. The ground is DRY in panels 1 to 3; light rain begins only in panel 4, wetting the pavement.

Vehicles (every panel): the family car is image 2 (Family car (a pearl-silver 2019 BMW 520d four-door sedan)); the food three-wheeler is image 3. Where each one is, panel by panel: PANEL 1 is INSIDE the moving family car: the car is the place, its inside exactly as image 2's view 5 shows it; the street is seen only through the windows. PANELS 2 and 3: the family car is stopped at the kerb of the roadside, nose toward screen right, the same car as image 2. PANEL 4: the food three-wheeler is parked in the compact vending bay beside the lit shop under the blue awning, at the edge of the pavement, which the location sheet shows empty; the family car has driven on and is not in the panel.

Geography: one narrow Colombo pavement beside patched asphalt and an open kerb drain; the shuttered frontages, the two-storey building with the balcony and its lit shop under the blue awning stand along the pavement, with the vending bay beside the shop. The car stops at the kerb before the vending bay; Anudi's route runs screen left to right toward the bay.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. Someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, both hands on the rim. Nobody looks into or faces the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
