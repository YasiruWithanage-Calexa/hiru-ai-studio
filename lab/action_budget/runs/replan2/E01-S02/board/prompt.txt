A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 9 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 3 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a landscape 16:9 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same moment's world, painted together: the same people, props, weather, light and colour grade in every panel.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Anudi is the red mannequin; driver is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. Everything else (the place, the props, the light, the weather) is a real, photorealistic film still.

World (every panel): Paint one image containing nine panels in a 3-by-3 grid, read left to right and top to bottom, with unmarked gutters and no text, symbols or arrows. Anudi is a smooth red artist's mannequin and the driver is a smooth blue artist's mannequin, with consistent adult proportions throughout. Render the surroundings and props realistically: a silver family sedan with neutral upholstery, a dark smartphone, a structured brown handbag with a side pocket, and a navy umbrella. The handbag stays on Anudi's left forearm, the umbrella stays in her left hand, and the smartphone stays in her right hand. Colombo is under soft overcast daylight, with neutral greys, restrained colour and gentle contrast; the arrival pavement is mostly dry, followed by fine rain and modest wet reflections in the walking and final panels. The small food three-wheeler has a dark maroon body, a plain cream canopy and a stainless serving shelf. All vehicle plates, signs and screens are unmarked.

Geography: The route runs horizontally from screen left to screen right: the car arrival point is in roadside block 1, the food three-wheeler is farther right in block 2, and the office approach continues beyond it at screen right. The sedan faces right beside the office-side pavement. Exterior cameras remain on the building side of the walking route, looking across the pavement toward the car and roadway, so Anudi travels across the image rather than into depth. Across the road, a low pale concrete wall topped with green railings provides a landmark at frame left, first glimpsed through the car window and then seen behind the arrival point. The closed silver car remains at frame left when the view advances toward the food three-wheeler, connecting the two roadside blocks. Keep the food three-wheeler outside the framing until panel 7.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.
