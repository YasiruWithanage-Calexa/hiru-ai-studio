A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 9 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 3 panels across and 3 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a landscape 16:9 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same scene's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel. The weather and the ground (dry or wet, rain falling or not) are as each panel says: they can change during the scene, and a panel that says it is dry has no rain and no wet ground.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the floor plan of this scene seen from above, north up.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Nimal is the red mannequin; Saman is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. The place, the light and the weather are a real, photorealistic film still.

Props (every panel): every prop (two tea cups, land deed) and every small thing a person holds or carries is a plain, smooth, matte white clay stand-in of the real object: its real size and outline, and nothing else: no screen, buttons, logo, lettering, pattern or colour. A vehicle is never a clay stand-in: every vehicle is painted real, exactly as its vehicle plate shows it, with its real colours. Each stand-in is held, carried or set down exactly as the real thing would be. The place itself is real: its ground, walls, water, plants, what is built into it, painted as a real photograph, never as clay.

World (every panel): A small village tea shop at dusk: one wooden table, a window on the left wall, an oil lamp. Time of day: dusk. Light: Soft, fading dusk enters from the west window. The lit oil lamp supplies a small amber fill across the table. Preserve the same source directions and exposure continuity throughout.

Geography: The wooden table stands to the north of the scene. The light comes from the west window, to the north-west. The light comes from the oil lamp, to the north. Beat 1: Nimal is seated, 1 m west of the wooden table. Beat 1: Saman is seated, 1 m east of the wooden table. Beat 2: Nimal is leaning in, 1 m west of the wooden table. Beat 3: Nimal is rising from the seat, 1 m west of the wooden table. Beat 4: Nimal is standing, 1 m west of the wooden table. Every camera stays on the same side of the action, so each person keeps their side of the frame from panel to panel.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Bodies and things (every panel): every mannequin has exactly two arms and two hands, each hand where the panel says. A held stand-in is gripped by the hand the panel names, its handle or edge inside the closed fingers; nothing hangs or floats in the air. Feet stand flat on the ground the panel names. A bag hangs from the shoulder or forearm the panel names.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.

Image 1 is the floor plan of this scene seen from above, north up, drawn without words: blue is water, brown is earth or a path, green is field, grey is concrete or stone; each coloured dot is where the mannequin of that colour is, a dashed line with an arrowhead is a walk, and each white triangle is a camera position pointing the way it looks. Take from it where everyone and everything stands relative to each other and to the place, and paint every panel to agree with it from that panel's own camera.
