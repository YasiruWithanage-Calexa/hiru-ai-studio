A cinematic storyboard sheet for a Sri Lankan television drama: 12 photorealistic film stills of ONE scene, laid out in a grid 4 panels across and 3 down, read left to right, then top to bottom. Every panel is a landscape 16:9 still of the same size, separated by thin, even, plain white gutters. All panels are the same scene's world, painted together: the same people, props, light and colour grade in every panel. The weather and the ground (dry or wet, rain falling or not) are as each panel says: they can change during the scene, and a panel that says it is dry has no rain and no wet ground.

Reference images, in order: image 1 is the location sheet for Mahawewa Canal: six views of the one place.

People (every panel): every person is a smooth, matte, faceless artist's mannequin in one solid colour, with jointed limbs and simple hands: Aruni is the red mannequin; Nimal is the blue mannequin. A small raised ridge on the front of the head, where a nose would be, shows which way the head faces. Each mannequin has the person's real height and build and sits, stands and holds things exactly as a real person would. No faces, no hair, no clothes, no skin: only the mannequin colour. The place, the light and the weather are a real, photorealistic film still.

Props (every panel): every prop (small medical bag, small metal key, phone) and every small thing a person holds or carries is a plain, smooth, matte white clay stand-in of the real object: its real size and outline, and nothing else: no screen, buttons, logo, lettering, pattern or colour. A vehicle is never a clay stand-in: every vehicle is painted real, exactly as its vehicle plate shows it, with its real colours. Each stand-in is held, carried or set down exactly as the real thing would be. The place itself is real: its ground, walls, water, plants, what is built into it, painted as a real photograph, never as clay.

World (every panel): An exterior rural irrigation canal beside a paddy field, with a narrow red-earth embankment path, grass-tufted banks and a low concrete sluice structure. Muddy water fills the channel beneath a weathered metal gate. Heavy dawn rain veils the distance; dim natural light catches wet earth in muted amber and earthy red, with subdued green rice beyond. Time of day: dawn. Light: Soft, dim dawn skylight diffused by heavy rain. Northwest light gives faces gentle modelling and catches water on skin, concrete and earth. No artificial source.

Geography: The weathered metal sluice gate between low concrete cheeks stands to the north-west of the scene. The wet concrete lip beside the gate runs from the west to the south-west. The near edge of the muddy canal runs from the west to the east. The narrow red-earth embankment path runs from the east to the west. The light comes from the rain-filled dawn sky to the northwest, to the north-west. Beat 1: Aruni is walking, 6 m east of the weathered metal sluice gate between low concrete cheeks, and walks west 4 m along the narrow red-earth embankment path. Beat 1: Nimal is lying still, 1 m south of the weathered metal sluice gate between low concrete cheeks. Beat 2: Aruni is walking, 3 m south-east of the weathered metal sluice gate between low concrete cheeks, and walks west 1 m along the narrow red-earth embankment path. Beat 3: Aruni is kneeling, 2 m south-east of the wet concrete lip beside the gate. Every camera stays on the same side of the action, so each person keeps their side of the frame from panel to panel.

Bodies (every panel): each person sits or stands the way a real person does the task in front of them, never posed for the camera. The body faces the task and only the head turns: someone at a steering wheel sits squarely behind it, the wheel centred in front of the chest, back against the seat, both hands on the rim; someone at a desk sits square to it; someone at a stove faces the stove. A camera beside a person sees the body side-on, not the chest. Nobody looks into or faces the lens unless the panel says so. Head and eyes turn together: the eyes never roll hard to one side while the face points at the camera; a near-frontal face looks just past the lens.

Bodies and things (every panel): every mannequin has exactly two arms and two hands, each hand where the panel says. A held stand-in is gripped by the hand the panel names, its handle or edge inside the closed fingers; nothing hangs or floats in the air. Feet stand flat on the ground the panel names. A bag hangs from the shoulder or forearm the panel names.

Draw NO text of any kind: no panel numbers, no captions, no titles, no timestamps, no arrows, no labels, no borders other than the gutters. The people, place and everyday details are Sri Lankan.

Take from image 1 the place itself: the canal, the sluice gate, the path and the paddy, their materials and how they sit together. Where things are in each panel comes from that panel's words.
